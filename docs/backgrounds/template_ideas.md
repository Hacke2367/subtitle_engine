# Background template ideas for every song type

**Status:** ideas only, 2026-09-29. Nothing here is approved or built. The owner picks; each pick then gets code-drawn stills and a moving sample before it is final (H-032 process).

**How they were found:** one agent per song type (11 in all, two workflows). Each agent:
- researched what other channels already show;
- wrote about six candidates;
- questioned each one hard (is it right for this song type, is it common, does it grab the first second, would a viewer understand it, does it follow every owner rule, can code draw it, what is the payoff);
- kept the best three.

Claude reviewed all 33 ideas. **★** marks Claude's pick per type. Raw agent output: `songs/_review/backgrounds/ideas_all.json` (gitignored).

## Claude's review across all types

- **Human figures are the biggest risk.** These ideas animate human shadows or silhouettes:
  - a soldier;
  - dancers;
  - a rap cypher;
  - a mother and daughter;
  - a caravan;
  - friends in a tent.
  Code-drawn moving people can look like shadow puppets. Each needs a moving sample before it can be judged.
- **Cloth is the hardest thing to draw by code.** Five ideas depend on cloth:
  - Udta dupatta;
  - Chhat ka dupatta;
  - Uska dupatta;
  - Aanchal (saree);
  - Parda uthe (veil).
  One cloth test (spring-and-point cloth) would settle all five at once. Only one dupatta look should be built anyway.
- **Repeats to avoid:**
  - the moon would become the third moonlit look;
  - the old cinema hall came up for both classics and sad, so it goes to classics;
  - trains came up again.
- **The strongest ideas let the song drive a visible story.** Each has a payoff the viewer waits for, and each works without people or colour props:
  - the power cut and return on the drop (hip-hop);
  - one stepwell step per sung word (motivational);
  - one leaf per sung word (sad);
  - a road of light written by the song (journey);
  - a fountain choreographed by the beats (party);
  - a lattice's light walking to dawn (Sufi).

### Chaand ka ghoonghat ★ (agent score 8)

A big soft moon hides behind slow, silver-edged clouds. It peeks out on each marked word, and on the last line the clouds open like a lifted veil and it stays out.

- **Scene:** Far: a deep navy night (about #060a18 at the top to #101a33 lower down) with one big moon high in the frame (centre x 540, y about 450). The disc is about 170 px, soft cream-silver and never pure white, with a wide halo. A thin high veil of cloud covers it, so at first only its glow shows. Mid: large cloud banks across the whole sky, with dark slate bellies and silver edges wherever the moon is behind them. When a thin cloud crosses the moon, a very faint pastel ring (rose, pale green, lilac: the real moon corona) forms round it. Near: low dark wisps of cloud, blurred, crossing faster. Along the bottom are the soft dark tops of trees, swaying, which get a silver rim only when the moon is out. The lyrics sit in the middle (y about 1000-1400) over the darkest band of cloud under the moon, with a gentle scrim. The moon always stays above the text. Colours: navy, slate, silver and cream; the corona is the only colour. Text themes: Soft Romantic v2, Cinematic, Lofi.
- **Motion:** Three layers of cloud drift right to left, each at its own speed (far slowest, near fastest), and slowly change shape, thinning and thickening. The moon stays where it is. The treetops sway gently. Nothing moves faster than a slow breath.
- **Song reaction:** Each sung word: the veil over the moon thins a little and its glow swells softly, then settles, so the moon breathes with the singer. In instrumental gaps the clouds close in again. Marked word: the clouds part around the moon for 2-3 s. It peeks out clearly, every cloud edge lights silver, the treetops get a silver rim and the lyrics catch a faint silver tint (the existing bounded tint). Then the clouds drift back: lukka-chuppi. Line starts: no event. Beats: nothing, no pulsing. Drops: a gust, and the near clouds race across for a moment. Arc: the clouds are thickest at the first line and thin over the song, so each peek shows a little more. Last line: the clouds open wide around the moon like a ghoonghat being lifted, and the moon stays out while silver light spreads over the whole sky and the treetops. If the first marked word comes late, a small peek happens in the first 2 s anyway, so something happens at once.
- **Payoff:** The veil lifts. After a whole song of peeks, the moon shows its full face on the last line and stays. The last frame (a full moon in a clearing of silver-edged clouds over dark treetops) is the best frame and the thumbnail. Sad mood for later: the reverse, where the clouds close over it for good on the last line.
- **Risks:** It can read as a stock moon clip for the first half-second. A too-bright moon becomes the hot spot the owner hates, so its brightness must be capped. If the clouds part in a neat circle it looks fake; the parting must follow the cloud shapes. It would be the third moonlit look in the catalogue. Songs with few marked words get fewer peeks.
- **Claude's note:** Strong and simple. It would be the third moonlit look (fog, kinaare ke nishaan), so keep it for songs where the moon matters.

### Udta dupatta (agent score 7.5)

A sheer rose dupatta streams across the top of the frame, close to the lens, over an Indian town's rooftops at dusk. It moves with the singing and flares on marked words, and on the last line the wind lets it go and it floats down over the lens.

- **Scene:** Far: a dusk sky just after sunset. Deep indigo at the top gives way to dusky violet, then a soft peach-rose afterglow low at the horizon (y about 1350), with a few long thin clouds holding the last pink. Mid: the town's flat rooftops in three hazy layers (water tanks, stair rooms and railings as soft dark silhouettes, the far ones pale in the haze). There are no lit windows, because far away they would be small hot points. Near: the dupatta, a long rose chiffon with a thin matte gold border. It is very close to the camera and out of focus, held off-frame at the top-left, and streams right across the top third (y about 0-700). The afterglow shows through it, so it glows soft rose where it is thin and deepens in its folds. At the very bottom is the dark edge of our own parapet. The lyrics sit in the calm band between the dupatta and the rooftops (y about 900-1300) over plain violet sky, with a gentle scrim. Text theme: Soft Romantic v2 is safest; Cinematic also works.
- **Motion:** Waves run slowly along the dupatta from the held end to the free end, and patches of breeze lift parts of it. It never stops and never flaps fast. The thin clouds drift very slowly, and the sky deepens from dusk to night over the song.
- **Song reaction:** Each sung word is a breath of wind: the ripples swell a little while the word is sung, so the cloth moves with the voice. In instrumental gaps it almost settles, as if listening. Marked word: a warm gust. The dupatta flares wide and lifts, and the afterglow shines through its full spread and washes the upper sky rose for a moment before it settles. Line starts: no event. Beats: nothing visible. Drops: a stronger gust sweeps it higher and wider. Arc: dusk turns into night. Last line: the held end slips free and the dupatta floats slowly down over the lens. For the last seconds the town is seen through rose chiffon, with the last line crisp on top.
- **Payoff:** The dupatta lands on the viewer: the filmi moment where the heroine's dupatta flies onto the hero, seen through the viewer's own eyes. The final frame (the town through rose chiffon, the last line sharp) is warm and made for sharing. Calmer or sad mood: it flies up and away into the night instead.
- **Risks:** The biggest risk: if the cloth is not convincing in motion it turns into clip-art, so a 10-second moving sample must come before anything else. The free end must never wander into the lyrics, which needs the guiding pull plus the legibility log. The final drift over the lens tints the whole background, so the lyrics must keep 3:1 contrast. My test's rooftops looked like flat vector blocks and need more haze, softer edges and fewer details. It is the heaviest look per frame (about 1 s).
- **Claude's note:** Needs convincing cloth; see the cloth note. Only one dupatta look should be built across all types.

### Do shaakhen (agent score 7)

Two old trees at the edges of a misty field before dawn each reach one branch toward the other. Every sung word grows them a little, and on the song's last word the tips touch as the first light of dawn blooms right where they meet.

- **Scene:** Far: a pre-dawn sky, deep blue at the top fading to lilac, with a faint peach glow low in the middle at the horizon (y about 900) where the sun will rise, and a distant tree line in mist. Mid: two big trees as dark silhouettes, one trunk at the left edge and one at the right, with crowns in the top corners. From each, one long branch reaches toward the centre at about y 450-750, above the lyrics, and the gap between the tips is visible from the first frame. Leaves are soft clusters, never single leaves. Near: tall grass and a few wildflower stalks along the bottom, dark and blurred and swaying, with bands of low mist drifting across the field. Colours: indigo, lilac, mist grey and peach, turning soft gold at the end. The lyrics sit over the dark misty field below the horizon (y about 1000-1400), with a gentle scrim. Text themes: Cinematic, Soft Romantic v2.
- **Motion:** Grass sways, the mist drifts in slow bands and the leaves stir. The two reaching branches grow slowly and smoothly, like a time-lapse, never in jumps. The sky brightens gradually over the song.
- **Song reaction:** Each sung word adds a little growth to the reaching branches, mostly on the side of the screen where that word sits, so the singing builds the bridge. In instrumental gaps the growth rests. Marked word: a flush of new leaves unfurls along the tips and catches the dawn glow with a soft warm rim. Beats: the leaves and grass sway a touch on the beat. Drops: a breeze runs through both crowns and the mist. Arc: the engine plans the growth so the gap closes exactly on the last word, whatever the song's length, and the viewer watches the gap shrink. Last word: the tips touch and interlace, and at that moment the sun's first light rises at the horizon under the meeting point. A soft gold glow blooms through the joined leaves and the mist.
- **Payoff:** 'Will they touch?' is on screen from the first second, and the answer lands on the last word together with the dawn. The final frame, two trees joined into a natural arch over the lyrics and lit from behind, is the thumbnail. Longing mood: the tips stop a finger's width apart on the last word, and the dawn still comes.
- **Risks:** Its first-second hook is weaker than the moon or the dupatta. Growth in visible steps would look mechanical, so each word's growth must ease in. The owner may see it as milan in a new costume. The dawn at the end must not wash out the lyrics, so the text stays over the dark field, not the glow. A song with very few words gives fewer, larger growth steps.
- **Claude's note:** Close to milan in spirit; weakest first second.

### Chhat ka dupatta (agent score 8.5)

A night terrace in a sleeping Indian town: one sheer dupatta left on the clothesline flutters in the wind with the moon glowing through it, and on the last line the wind carries it away across the moon.

- **Scene:** Far: a deep indigo night sky (#03050b at the top to #151a30 lower down). A large moon (#e6ecff) sits in the upper middle behind thin, slow clouds; the sky is brightest around it and darkens towards the town. Mid: the rooftops of a sleeping Indian town in blue haze, two or three rows, each fainter than the one in front: flat roofs with broken parapets, black water tanks, stair rooms, a tin shed, a neem tree between the houses, old TV antennas. Two windows are still awake behind curtains, only a faint amber glow (#ffb46a), never a point of light. Near: our own terrace. The parapet wall runs along the bottom with moonlight on its top edge, an iron pole stands at the left edge, and a clothesline crosses the upper sky. On it hangs one long sheer dupatta, dusty rose (#c98a95) with a thin dull-gold zari border and small tassels; where it passes in front of the moon, the moon glows rose through the cloth. It is the only colour in the frame. Lyrics: the middle band (about y 850 to 1400) sits on dark sky above the far roofs, with a gentle scrim. The dupatta stays above about y 820 and the town below about y 1400.
- **Motion:** The dupatta never stops moving: slow waves run down its length to the free end, which curls and turns. The clouds drift slowly over the moon, haze drifts between the rooftops, and the line sags and bounces a little with each gust. Everything is breeze-slow; a gust rises and settles over 1 to 2 seconds. The video opens mid-flutter, so the first frame already moves.
- **Song reaction:** Each sung word: a breath of wind; a small wave runs down the dupatta to its tip. Marked word: a gust; the dupatta billows up across the moon (the moon shines rose through it) and slowly settles. Lines: each gust leaves it a little further along the line, so over the song it creeps towards the end of the line. Beats: nothing, it stays calm. Drops: a strong gust; the line bounces and the clouds clear off the moon for a moment. Last line: the wind lifts it off the line; it floats up across the moon and out of the top of the frame, and the empty line keeps swaying.
- **Payoff:** The last frame is an empty clothesline swaying under the moon over the sleeping town: the last thing of hers is gone. People watch to the end because the dupatta keeps slipping along the line with every gust, and they can feel it is going to go.
- **Risks:** 1) Cloth movement: if it looks like a stiff ribbon or flag it becomes clip-art. Judge a moving sample first; the fallback is simple cloth physics (points joined by springs) instead of drawn waves. 2) Rooftops: in the test they read as clean black boxes; they need broken parapets, tanks, cables, a tree and haze. 3) The cloth must stay above the lyrics; in the test its tip touched the faded previous line. 4) A second moon after kinaare; it can be swapped for the town's glow in the sky. 5) Colour: red would read as bridal or festive, so keep it dusty rose, lilac or off-white. 6) Its flight on the last line must be slow and believable, not a sudden vanish.
- **Claude's note:** Best metaphor of the sad set, but it depends on the cloth test.

### Aakhri patta ★ (agent score 7.5)

One big old tree at the end of dusk: every sung word shakes one leaf loose, the tree empties with the song, and the last leaf, hanging there from the first second, falls on the last word.

- **Scene:** Far: the sky just after sunset. High clouds are still lit dusty rose at the top (#e0a090 on #382a36), deepening to night blue through the middle (#141a2b to #0c1020), with a thin ember line (#d88a52) low on the horizon above a far village tree line in haze. Mid: one big old tree with a round Indian crown (neem-like, not a flat-topped acacia). Its trunk stands at the left edge and its crown fills the top third: a dark silhouette (#07060a) with dusty rose on its upper edges and ember light on its lower edges. The leaves are sparse, as late in the season. One leaf hangs apart at the tip of a thin twig in the upper middle, catching a little more light: the last leaf. Near: the ground along the bottom, covered with fallen leaves that catch the ember light; now and then a big out-of-focus leaf drifts past close to the camera at the edge of the frame. Lyrics: the calm night-blue band in the middle (y 850 to 1400), kept free of branches.
- **Motion:** Leaves flutter down slowly, 2 to 5 seconds each, swinging side to side and turning, slower at the end of each swing, the way real leaves fall. The crown sways gently, low mist creeps along the horizon, and over the song the rose afterglow fades to deep blue. Even during a long intro a few leaves fall on their own, so the first second moves.
- **Song reaction:** Each sung word: one leaf lets go and flutters down. The tree holds exactly as many leaves as the song has sung words, plus the last leaf. Marked word: a small gust; the crown sways, a few leaves go together, and one passes close to the camera, big and soft. Beats: nothing. Drops: a strong gust, and many leaves swirl across the frame at once. Lines: no separate event, since the words are the leaves. Arc: the crown thins and the ground fills as the song goes on, and the afterglow fades to night. Last line: on the last word the last leaf lets go and drifts down past the lyrics to the ground.
- **Payoff:** The last frame is a bare tree against the night, the ground full of leaves: the song has counted it down. People watch to the end because the lone last leaf is visible from the start and they want to see whether it falls. An optional hopeful version for waiting songs: the last leaf holds, as in O. Henry's 'The Last Leaf', which many Indians read at school.
- **Risks:** 1) Stock look: a tree at sunset is common. What sets it apart is the countdown, and a scrolling viewer only feels that after a few seconds. 2) In the test, a flat-topped crown on a flat horizon looked like an African savanna acacia; it needs a round neem-like crown and an Indian field or village edge. 3) Bare branches at night can look spooky: keep it dusk, keep some leaves until late, and never put a full moon behind bare branches. 4) Falling leaves are hard to see over the dark lyric band; they read mostly against the lit upper sky. 5) Fast songs mean many leaves, so cap the rate. 6) One thing falling per word works the same way as the rain look.
- **Claude's note:** Pure engine idea: the song counts the leaves. Needs an Indian (neem-like) tree, not an acacia.

### Jaagti raat (agent score 7)

Lying awake at night, looking up: the ceiling fan turns slowly overhead, the streetlight throws the window grill's shadow and swaying neem leaves onto the ceiling, passing cars sweep light across it, and dawn comes on the last line.

- **Scene:** Far: the night outside, seen through the window at the bottom of the frame: a sodium streetlight's warm glow (#ffb46b) through neem leaves, and a deep blue sky. Mid: the room seen from the bed, looking up. The dark wall holds the window (an old iron grill with a half-circle pattern, a sheer curtain on one side); above the corner, the ceiling (dark blue-grey plaster, #2a3a66 in very low light) carries all the light: the grill's long, soft, warm pattern and the moving shadows of the leaves. When a car passes, a cooler white copy of the pattern (#dfe8ff) sweeps across. Near: the ceiling fan above the bed, top right, its cream blades (#cfc6b4, faintly lit by the window) blurred by their turning, its hub dark. Lyrics: the dark middle band (the ceiling just in front of the window), calm between the fan above and the window below. The sweeps stay mostly above it.
- **Motion:** The fan turns slowly, always softly blurred. The leaf shadows on the ceiling sway gently and the curtain stirs. When a car passes, its light slides across the ceiling in 2 to 3 seconds, stretching as it goes, exactly as it does in a real room, and the fan's shadow swings round with it. The video opens on a sweep, so the first second moves.
- **Song reaction:** Each sung word: the neem outside stirs, so the leaf shadows on the ceiling tremble a little. Marked word: a car passes; its white light sweeps across the ceiling, the room lights up for a moment and the fan's shadow swings across, then the dark returns. Drops: a truck gives a slower, bigger, warmer sweep, and the grill's shadow trembles. Long gaps between lines (instrumental parts): a far car's faint sweep keeps the room alive. Beats: nothing. Arc: traffic thins as the night goes on, and near the end the sky in the window turns from black to deep blue. Last line: dawn. Grey-blue light fills the window and the ceiling, the streetlight switches off and its warm pattern fades, and the fan keeps turning.
- **Payoff:** The last frame is the same ceiling in pale dawn light, the fan still turning: the whole night went by without sleep. The ending is the reverse of khaali jagah's light going out: here the light comes, and it brings no comfort.
- **Risks:** 1) Dim: a night room is dark by nature, and the owner rejected the room partly for being dim. 2) It reads more slowly than the other two in a still; it needs a clear fan, a clear window and a sweep in the first second. 3) It may remind the owner of the rejected sunlit room and rainy window. 4) The fan must turn softly; a crisp spinning fan looks mechanical. 5) The ceiling is one flat surface, so depth depends on the fan in front and the view outside. 6) Car sweeps crossing the lyrics would disturb reading; keep them mostly above.
- **Claude's note:** Relatable, but dim, and it is a room. The owner rejected dim rooms before.

### Aakhri show ★ (agent score 8)

An empty old single-screen cinema at night, seen from the balcony. A film plays to no one: the projector's beam passes over your head and the screen's light rolls over rows of empty seats. On the last line the film ends and the dim house lights come up on the empty hall.

- **Scene:** FAR (top third):
- The big screen at the far end (about y 300-580).
- It glows with the light of a film, but no picture: soft warm and cool light that changes like scenes.
- Dark maroon velvet curtains at its sides; the side walls run toward it; a few pillars catch its light.

MID:
- The projector's beam comes from behind and above the viewer.
- It fills the top of the frame as a wide fan of silver-blue light narrowing onto the screen, with dust drifting slowly inside it.
- Below the screen, the stalls: curved rows of empty seats running down toward us.
- Only their tops catch the screen's light, as faint maroon and warm edges: brighter near the screen, darker near us.
- Option for the couple: two seats in the middle are the only ones folded down.

NEAR:
- The balcony ledge at the bottom (y 1780+), out of focus, with a brass rail catching one thin line of light.

COLOURS: near-black teal-navy, a silver-blue beam, cream and cool screen light, dim maroon seats. Dark and cinematic, like a film still.

LYRICS: y 870-1270, over the middle rows of dark seats, with a gentle scrim. The screen and beam stay in the top third, above the words.
- **Motion:** - Dust drifts through the beam in slow swirls, like smoke, never sparkles.
- The screen's light changes slowly, as if the film is moving from scene to scene, and that light rolls softly over the seat tops and the curtain folds.
- The projector's flutter is a slow breath, never a strobe.
- A very slow push toward the screen over the whole song: the near rows slide down faster than the far ones, so the depth is felt.
- **Song reaction:** - Each sung word: the screen's light rises a touch with the voice, and the seat tops brighten with it.
- Marked word: a 'cut'. The screen changes to a new scene colour (for example cool blue to warm amber), the whole hall's light changes with it and the beam flares softly. Optionally the two folded-down seats catch that light for a moment.
- New line: a slow crossfade of the screen's light, like a new shot.
- Beats: none, or only a faint breath in the projector's flutter.
- Drop: the screen goes bright for a moment and washes the whole hall in soft light, then settles.
- Over the song: the dust in the beam slowly thickens.
- Last line: the screen fades to black and the beam dies. Then warm, dim house lights slowly come up as soft washes on the walls.
- **Payoff:** The film is over. The last frame is the empty hall in dim warm house light: rows and rows of empty seats, dust still hanging in the air, a blank grey screen. It is the one sad look where light comes UP at the end, and what it shows is emptiness: 'picture khatam, hum bhi'. Viewers stay to see the lights come on.
- **Risks:** - The seat rows can look like clip-art icons. They must be irregular, soft and blurred with depth; test in a moving sample first.
- The screen can look like a dead grey slab if its light doesn't change enough.
- The screen's changing colours must stay 'scene cuts', never drift into the rejected flowing-gradient look.
- The projector flutter must stay a slow breath, never a flicker that pokes the eye.
- The owner may feel 'room again' after the rejected room.
- The couple is implied, not shown (the two folded-down seats are an option to make it visible).
- **Claude's note:** Same place as Purani Talkies (classics); give the cinema to classics.
