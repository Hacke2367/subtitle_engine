## The idea

A monsoon evening over open fields. A heavy dark cloud mass rolls in over a last warm band of light
at the horizon; a telegraph line runs away across the fields; the wind moves when the voice moves;
the first rain falls on the last line ("barsaat aa gayi").

## Layers

- **Clouds**: a high mass and a lower shelf (faster, nearer), built from big lobes; lit only from
  below by the band, so only the undersides that hang lowest catch the warm light; the lobes' form
  shows in soft value changes. Thin dark scud drifts across the band.
- **Band**: amber with a little rose under the cloud base; it narrows as the clouds come down.
- **Land**: one ragged tree line on the horizon with a few broad mango/neem crowns, dark fields with
  thin strips of flooded paddy mirroring the band.
- **Telegraph line**: ten poles with two crossarms running away to the left, sagging wires, all
  below the lyrics.
- **Grass**: tall dark grass along the bottom, its tips catching the band.

## Motion and song reaction

- Clouds drift left to right at two speeds; the shelf's base moves a little.
- Word: a gust runs through the grass from left to right and the wires swing.
- Line: the cloud base comes a step lower over the band.
- Marked word: a soft glow inside the clouds, like lightning far away, lighting the lobes from
  within; it swells and fades over ~1.6 s, never a flash.
- Last line: slow colourless rain at several depths (thinner behind the lyrics), and the last of the
  band goes out under the clouds.
- Without words: steady gusts, the clouds come down over the whole length.

## Known issues

- The upper clouds are soft; up close they lean toward smoke rather than crisp cumulus edges.
- The grass reads as dry reeds more than lush monsoon grass.
- The telegraph line is small (it stays below the lyrics), so the "golden era" cue is quiet.
